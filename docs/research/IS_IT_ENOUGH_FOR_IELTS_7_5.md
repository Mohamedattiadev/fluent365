# Is Fluent365 + AI speaking apps enough for IELTS General Training 7.5 in 365 days?

Learner profile: about B1 now, Turkish/Arabic background, 1–1.5 h/day, target IELTS **General Training (GT) 7.5 overall** in about 365 days.
Plan under review: Fluent365 (as of `docs/DEVELOPMENT_STATUS.md`, 2026-11-16) plus AI speaking apps (Praktika, possibly Speak, ELSA, Gliglish, ChatGPT voice).
All web sources were accessed **2026-11-16**. Markers: **[V]** = read on the page itself; **[S]** = seen only in a search-result summary/abstract (treat figures as approximate); **[J]** = the author's own judgment, not a measured result.

---

## 0. Verdict (short)

**Conditional no.** As it stands, the plan is enough to make large, real progress (a realistic outcome is **6.5, possibly 7.0**), but it is **not enough to reach 7.5 reliably**. Three things are missing that no amount of app drilling replaces:

1. **Input volume.** About 15–20% of the needed reading and listening exposure is in the plan. Fluent365's reading is short and its listening uses device TTS. 7.5 needs hundreds of hours of authentic audio and several hundred thousand words of reading.
2. **Calibrated feedback on writing (and to a lesser extent speaking).** Writing is the hardest skill for Arabic-L1 candidates and is self-assessed only in Fluent365. AI speaking apps do not score against IELTS descriptors.
3. **Full, timed GT mock tests.** There are 2 listening tests and 1 reading mock. 7.5 needs about 10–16 full timed mocks under exam conditions.

The 1–1.5 h/day budget is also at the very bottom of what the hour estimates allow for a 2.5-band jump. **With the additions in §6–§8 (mostly free, plus a small paid budget) and about 30–45 min/day of extra "dead-time" listening, 7.5 becomes a realistic stretch goal and 7.0 becomes likely.** [J]

Rough odds [J — a judgment from the hour estimates in §1, not a measured probability]:

| Scenario | 6.5 overall | 7.0 overall | 7.5 overall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan as-is (Fluent365 + speaking app, 1–1.5 h/day ≈ 365–550 h) | likely (~70%) | possible (~35–45%) | unlikely (~10–20%) |
| Plan + additions in this report (≈ 1.5 h focused + 0.5–0.75 h passive listening ≈ 700–800 h) | very likely | likely (~65–75%) | realistic stretch (~35–50%) |
| Same, but baseline turns out to be "high B1" (already IELTS 5.5–6.0 on a diagnostic) | near certain | very likely | ~50–65% |

The single biggest unknown is the true starting point. **Take a diagnostic in week 1** (§9).

## 1. How big is the jump? Hours and timelines

### 1.1 Level mapping
- IELTS 7.0 ≈ CEFR C1, and IELTS 4.5 ≈ B1, per Cambridge's own Write & Improve help centre [V] (https://help.writeandimprove.com/en/articles/1216877-cefr-versus-ielts). IELTS 7.5 sits in the middle of C1.
- So "B1 → 7.5" is roughly **B1 → mid-C1**, i.e. about **2.5–3 IELTS bands** from a typical B1 score of 4.5–5.0. If the learner is "high B1" (about 5.0–5.5), it is 2–2.5 bands.

### 1.2 Guided learning hours
- Cambridge cumulative guided learning hours from zero: B1 ≈ 350–400 h, B2 ≈ 500–600 h, C1 ≈ 700–800 h [V, already cited in `HOW_PEOPLE_LEARN_LANGUAGES.md` §1]. **B1 → C1 ≈ 300–450 guided hours**, and mid-C1 needs more than the bottom of that range.
- "About 200 hours per band" is widely repeated by prep sites and attributed to Cambridge/British Council, but **no primary page confirming it was found** [S] (e.g. https://www.ielts.international/fr/how-long-to-prepare-for-ielts , https://ielts.net/how-long-to-increase-1-point-ielts). Applied naively, 2.5 bands ≈ 500 h of *guided* study, plus independent work.
- "Guided" hours mean taught lessons plus set tasks. Independent input (reading, podcasts) is extra. Self-study apps without a teacher usually convert hours into progress less efficiently than taught hours [J].

### 1.3 Observed band gains
- **ielts.org:** two studies found that students on IELTS/English courses took on average **about three months to improve by up to half a band**. The biggest gains came from students starting below 5.5 who studied **more than 23 hours a week** and were more motivated. One student studying about 60 h/week for three months gained 1.0 band. [V] https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/how-long-will-it-take-to-get-the-ielts-band-score-i-need
- **Elder & O'Loughlin (IELTS Research Report vol. 4, report 6):** 112 students on 10–12-week intensive courses in Australia/New Zealand gained **about half a band overall**, with variable progress; **listening gained most, reading least.** [V] https://ielts.org/researchers/our-research/research-reports/investigating-the-relationship-between-intensive-english-language-study-and-band-score-gain-on-ielts
- **Green (2005, *Assessing Writing*):** 476 learners on 3–10-week EAP courses gained only about **0.2 of a band in Writing** [S] (search summary of the paper; https://www.euroslajournal.org/article/10.22599/jesla.74/). Writing moves slowest.
- **Arabic-L1 baseline:** ielts.org's 2019 first-language table (Academic + GT combined) reported Arabic speakers' means as **L 5.7, R 5.4, W 5.2, S 5.9, overall 5.6** [S] (https://ielts.org/researchers/our-research/test-statistics; the 2024–25 per-L1 data is in the XLSX linked there and was not opened). No Turkish row was seen. **Writing is the weakest skill for Arabic speakers on average.**

### 1.4 What this means for 365–550 hours
- 365–550 h ≈ the *guided* hours for B1 → C1. It is **just enough on paper, only if the hours are high-quality and supported by a lot of extra input** (which the guided-hours figures assume).
- Intensive-course data (~0.5 band per 3 months at 20+ h/week) suggests a full-time student gains ~2 bands per year. Our learner studies about 1/3 of that per week but for 12 months. Expect **~1.5–2 bands** from the core plan alone [J]. From 5.0 that lands at **6.5–7.0**.
- **The way to 7.5 is volume:** push total hours to ~700–800 by adding passive/semi-active listening and reading in spare time (commute, chores, before sleep), which costs little willpower.

### 1.5 The score arithmetic that makes 7.5 reachable
The overall band is the mean of four skills rounded to the nearest half band; .25 rounds up to .5 and .75 rounds up to the next whole band [V] (https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-scoring-in-detail). So 7.5 overall needs an **average ≥ 7.25**, e.g.:

| L | R (GT) | W | S | Mean | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.0 | 8.0 | 6.5 | 6.5 | 7.25 | **7.5** |
| 8.0 | 7.5 | 6.5 | 7.0 | 7.25 | **7.5** |
| 7.5 | 7.5 | 6.5 | 7.0 | 7.125 | 7.0 |

Raw-score anchors (averages; vary by test version) [V, same ielts.org page]: Listening band 7 ≈ 30/40, band 8 ≈ 35/40; **GT Reading band 7 ≈ 35/40** (GT reading needs more correct answers than Academic). Third-party charts put GT Reading 7.5 at ~36/40 and 8.0 at ~37–38/40 [S] (https://galvanizetestprep.com/ielts-reading-band-score/).

**Strategy implication:** The receptive skills (L and R) are where 8.0 is most achievable for a disciplined self-studier, and they carry W and S. **Target L 8, R 8, W 6.5–7, S 7.** This is the realistic route for an Arabic/Turkish-L1 learner whose writing will likely lag.

## 2. AI speaking apps: what the evidence says

| Tool | What independent evidence exists | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Praktika** | No controlled study found. Two small Indonesian qualitative studies of learner/teacher perceptions [S] (https://e-journal.stkipsiliwangi.ac.id/index.php/eltin/article/view/5857/2212). Its own help page describes "helpful tips and subtle corrections" on grammar, pronunciation, vocabulary and pace, with **no scoring method and no IELTS calibration described** [V] (https://intercom.help/praktika-ai/en/articles/11684894-feedback-features). A 2026 review notes the pronunciation/fluency score formula is not published [S] (https://copycatcafe.com/blog/praktika-review-2026). | Good for **speaking volume and confidence**. Unverified for band gains. |
| **Speak** | No efficacy study for the speak.com app found (results were for unrelated "Speak Agent") [S]. | Same as Praktika: volume tool. |
| **ELSA Speak** | Several small studies (15–50 learners, often quasi-experimental, mostly Indonesia/Iran/Taiwan) and a 2020–24 systematic review report pronunciation gains [S] (https://callej.org/index.php/journal/article/view/519 ; https://core.ac.uk/outputs/674026906). **But** a study of ELSA, Loora and Vocal Image found app feedback **"frequently inaccurate and often limited to consonant and vowel sounds"** [S] (Journal of Second Language Pronunciation, https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/jslp.24053.son). | Useful for **segmental drills** (specific sounds). Do not trust its "% native-like" score as an IELTS pronunciation band. |
| **Gliglish** | A comparison site quotes "over 75% improvement" from an 8-week study by other authors; not verified [S]. | Treat as unverified. |
| **ChatGPT voice / other LLM voice** | No IELTS-specific speaking study found. General chatbot meta-analyses report **large pooled effects on speaking (g ≈ 1.0)**, but from small sets of mostly short classroom studies with high risk of bias [S] (e.g. https://cedtech.editorialpark.com/article/the-effectiveness-of-artificial-intelligence-in-english-instruction-for-speaking-and-listening-17310). | Most flexible: can role-play an IELTS examiner for Parts 1–3 and give a transcript to correct. Band estimates are not reliable. |

**ASR for pronunciation in general.** Ngo, Chen & Lai (2023/2024, *ReCALL* 36(1)) meta-analysed 15 studies (38 effects): **g = 0.69** for ASR over non-ASR instruction. Explicit corrective feedback (g = 0.86) beat indirect feedback (0.50). Segmental targets (0.82) beat suprasegmental ones (0.37). **1–4 weeks of use had ~no effect (0.07)**, while 5+ weeks did (0.72–1.01). **Using it alone (0.44) was weaker than with peers (0.89) or a teacher (1.24, two studies).** [V] https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/effectiveness-of-automatic-speech-recognition-in-eslefl-pronunciation-a-metaanalysis/A915444CF252B61D14961D2FE733822D

### What AI speaking apps do *not* give you
- **No band-calibrated score.** None of these apps publishes validation against IELTS examiners. Their scores measure "how well the recogniser understood you", not Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammar or Pronunciation as IELTS defines them.
- **Lenient conversation partner.** Bots adapt to you, rarely interrupt or push with Part 3 abstract follow-ups, and accept short answers. The IELTS Band 7 Fluency descriptor asks for speaking **at length without noticeable effort or loss of coherence** [S, paraphrased in https://ielts.com.au/australia/results/scores/speaking].
- **Recasts, not repair.** Feedback after the turn is mostly reformulation. The research in `HOW_PEOPLE_LEARN_LANGUAGES.md` §8 shows recasts produce the least self-repair. You must re-say the corrected sentence.
- **ASR accent tolerance.** Recognisers often "understand" errors that a human examiner would mark (and sometimes mark correct speech wrong).

### Best way to use them [J, built on the evidence above]
1. **Daily 15–20 min, 5+ weeks minimum** (short use shows no effect).
2. Use them for **volume and Part 1/Part 3 style questioning**; force long turns ("answer in at least 4 sentences; ask me why/how follow-ups").
3. **Record, transcribe, correct, re-say.** Ask ChatGPT voice for a transcript, paste it into Fluent365's Import Centre, add the 3 most important errors to the mistake notebook, then repeat the answer correctly.
4. ELSA (or similar) only for **specific sounds** you know are weak (§4.6), not as a band predictor.
5. **Once a month, a human** (teacher or ex-examiner) mock: the only way to calibrate the band.

## 3. What else 7.5 needs (evidence and what high scorers do)

### 3.1 Extensive reading volume
- Nation proposed **500,000 running words per year**, sustained for several years; most ER programmes recommend **200,000–300,000+ words/year** ≈ one graded reader a week [S] (summary of ER literature; Extensive Reading Foundation calculator: https://erfoundation.org/wordpress/?p=2615).
- Vocabulary size explains about **58% of the variance in overall IELTS bands** (Milton, Wade & Hopkins 2010) [S] (via https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/how-to-address-vocabulary-in-an-ielts-preparation-course and secondary blogs). Prep sources link B2–C1 / 6.5–7.5 with roughly **4,000–5,000+ word families** [S]. Fluent365's 5,200 entries are a good *deliberate* base, but depth (collocation, register, spelling) comes from reading.
- **Gap:** Fluent365's reading is short lesson texts and one mock. **Target: ~1,200–2,000 words/day (15–20 min) → 400,000–600,000 words in the year**, moving from graded readers (level 4–6) to news, GT-type texts (notices, adverts, workplace documents) and then real articles.

### 3.2 Extensive listening with real human audio
- Subtitled-TV countries show much higher English proficiency [V, cited in `HOW_PEOPLE_LEARN_LANGUAGES.md` §3]. Studies of extensive listening with podcasts report gains in listening and lower listening anxiety (e.g. Chang & Renandya 2023, 55 students) [S] (https://riset.unisma.ac.id/index.php/JREALL/article/download/21190/16071/63114).
- IELTS Listening uses varied **native accents** (British, Australian, North American, etc.), natural speed and distractors. Device TTS has none of the connected-speech features (weak forms, linking, elision) that Field identifies as the main decoding bottleneck.
- **Gap:** TTS-only listening. **Target: 30–45 min/day of authentic audio** (≈ 200–270 h/year), of which ~10 min is active (dictation/shadowing of a short clip) and the rest is extensive.

### 3.3 Writing feedback
- Corrective feedback has a medium, lasting effect (Li 2010) [V, cited in `HOW_PEOPLE_LEARN_LANGUAGES.md` §8]. Self-assessment with checklists cannot find errors the learner does not know are errors.
- **LLMs as scorers are unreliable for bands.** Uyar & Büyükahıska (2025, *IJATE*): ChatGPT scored IELTS Task 2 essays significantly differently from humans, generally lower [S] (https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/ijate/article/1496193). A ChatGPT-4o study found only "fair" QWK agreement [S]. Koraishi (2024) found high mean agreement but notable individual discrepancies and outliers, and advises against replacing human judgment [S] (https://eurokd.com/doi/10.32038/ltrq.2024.43.02). A 2026 arXiv paper: LLM–human agreement is "relatively weak", and LLMs **over-score short, under-developed essays** and under-score longer essays with minor errors [V] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23714).
- **Cambridge Write & Improve:** free tier gives automatic feedback and a **CEFR** level; the paid **+Test Zone** gives an **estimated IELTS band** for IELTS-style essays [V] (https://help.writeandimprove.com/en/articles/1216877-cefr-versus-ielts ; https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/learning-english/free-resources/write-and-improve/). It is built by Cambridge on past learner essays; no accuracy figures are published.
- **Practical rule:** Use LLMs for **error-finding and rewrites** (which they do well), use W&I for a **trend line**, and use a **human (ideally ex-examiner) once or twice a month** for the actual band. Writing moves slowest (§1.3), so this is where money is best spent.

### 3.4 Full timed mocks
- Official free material covers format but **contains no full-length GT Reading test** [V, `IELTS_OFFICIAL_MATERIALS.md` §3]. Full GT mocks come from the paid **Cambridge IELTS GT books** (4 tests each) [V, same doc].
- High scorers consistently report doing many full timed tests and *analysing every mistake* (question type, paraphrase missed, spelling). Plan **~12–16 full mocks**: Cambridge IELTS 15–20 GT gives 24 tests (Listening/Speaking shared with Academic, GT-specific Reading/Writing).

### 3.5 Speaking with a human examiner-like partner
- ASR feedback works better with a peer/teacher than alone (§2). An examiner-style partner applies time pressure, interrupts in Part 3 and judges pronunciation as *intelligibility and features*, not phoneme-match.
- **Minimum:** one 15–20 min human mock each month from month 4; weekly in the last 2 months.

### 3.6 Pronunciation for Arabic/Turkish speakers
Common problem areas (practitioner knowledge [J], verify with a teacher): Arabic L1 — /p/–/b/, /v/–/f/, vowel contrasts (/ɪ/–/e/, /ɒ/–/ʌ/), adding vowels into consonant clusters ("estreet"), spelling vowels in listening answers; Turkish L1 — /θ ð/ (→ t/d or s/z), /w/–/v/, vowel length, word and sentence stress. IELTS Pronunciation 7 is about **features** (stress, rhythm, chunking, intonation) and effort for the listener, so shadowing is the best general tool, with ELSA-type drills for specific sounds.

### 3.7 GT-specific tasks
- **Writing Task 1 = letter** (≥150 words, formal/semi-formal/informal), Task 2 essay (≥250 words, double weight) [V, `HOW_PEOPLE_LEARN_LANGUAGES.md` §11]. Tone/register control in letters is a frequent band limiter.
- **GT Reading:** Section 1 (notices, adverts, timetables), Section 2 (workplace texts: contracts, training, job descriptions), Section 3 (one long general-interest text). Sections 1–2 must be done fast to bank time for Section 3. Volume of *this text type* matters because GT Reading needs ~35/40 for band 7.

### 6.2 Paid (prioritised by value per money)
1. **Cambridge IELTS General Training books** (e.g. 17–20, newest first; 4 full tests each, with answer keys and audio). Buy legally; do not use pirated PDFs. Roughly 3–4 books cover all mocks needed.
2. **Human writing correction, 2×/month from month 3; weekly in the last 2 months** (an ex-examiner tutor on a tutoring marketplace, or an IELTS centre's correction service). Highest return for an Arabic/Turkish-L1 learner.
3. **Human speaking mock, 1×/month from month 4; weekly in the last 6–8 weeks.**
4. **Write & Improve +Test Zone** (monthly subscription, estimated IELTS band) in months 8–12 as a cheap trend line between human checks.
5. **Paid speaking app (Praktika or similar):** fine for daily volume; not a substitute for items 2–3.
6. Optional: an official computer-delivered practice test/IELTS Ready premium near the end.

### 6.3 Habits
- **Never skip input.** If time is short, keep FSRS reviews + 15 min input and drop new words.
- **Dead-time listening** 30–45 min/day (commute, chores, walking). This is what pushes total hours from ~500 to ~750.
- **Mistake log reviewed weekly**; rewrite every corrected essay once.
- **English-only phone/media** where possible (subtitles in English, not L1).

## 7. Recommended weekly schedule

Assumes 75–90 min of focused study per day + 30–45 min of passive/semi-active listening. Total ≈ 13–15 h/week ≈ 700–800 h/year.

### 7.1 Daily core (Mon–Fri)
| Block | Minutes | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Fluent365 guided session (reviews, new words, grammar, dictation, short output) | 40–45 | Fluent365 (60-min budget minus input slot) |
| Speaking app (examiner-style Q&A, long turns; correct + re-say 3 errors) | 15 | Praktika / ChatGPT voice |
| Extensive reading (graded → authentic → GT text types) | 15–20 | Books/news, logged in Fluent365 |
| Extensive listening (passive/semi-active, not counted above) | 30–45 | Podcasts/BBC/ABC, in dead time |

### 7.2 Weekly rotation
| Day | Extra focus (inside or replacing part of the core) |
|---|---|
| Mon | Writing Task 1 letter (20 min timed) → LLM/W&I feedback → rewrite |
| Tue | Listening: one Cambridge/official section timed + full mistake analysis; 10 min shadowing |
| Wed | Writing Task 2 (40 min timed) → feedback → rewrite next day |
| Thu | Reading: one GT section timed (Section 1/2 early in the year, Section 3 later) + question-type analysis |
| Fri | Speaking: full Part 1–3 recorded (11–14 min) → transcript → 3 errors to notebook; 4/3/2 on a repeated cue card |
| Sat (60–90 min) | Months 1–6: longer reading/listening + review week's errors. Months 7–12: **full timed mock** every 2–3 weeks (or 2 papers) |
| Sun | Light: FSRS reviews + enjoyable input only (series with English subtitles, a book). Weekly review of mistake notebook and input totals. |

### 7.3 Phases across the year
| Months | Phase | Emphasis | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | B1 → B1+/B2− foundation | 60% input/vocab/grammar, 25% output, 15% IELTS format | Graded readers (level 4–5), 6 Minute English level audio; one writing piece/week with feedback; no full mocks; diagnostic in week 1. |
| 4–7 | B2 build | 50% input, 30% output with feedback, 20% IELTS | Authentic news and podcasts; 2 writing pieces/week; first human writing and speaking checks; one full mock at end of month 6. |
| 8–10 | IELTS intensive (B2+ → C1−) | 40% input, 30% output, 30% exam practice | Full mock every 2–3 weeks; question-type weakness drills; 2 human writing corrections/month; monthly speaking mock. |
| 11–12 | Exam readiness | 30% input, 30% output, 40% mocks | Weekly full mocks (Cambridge books); weekly human writing feedback and speaking mock; book the exam when two consecutive mocks are ≥ target. |

## 8. Monthly checkpoints and target scores

Use Fluent365's monthly checkpoint (to be built, §5 #4) or: one official/Cambridge listening section, one GT reading section, one timed Task 2 (W&I level + human band when available), one recorded Part 2. Targets assume a baseline of ~5.0 overall; if the diagnostic is higher, shift the curve up.

| Month | Overall target | L | R (GT) | W | S | Other checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (week 1) | Diagnostic (~5.0?) | — | — | — | — | Vocab size test; W&I level; recorded Part 2 |
| 2 | 5.5 | 5.5–6.0 | 5.5–6.0 | 5.0–5.5 | 5.5 | 60k words read; 30 h listening |
| 4 | 6.0 | 6.5 | 6.5 | 5.5 | 5.5–6.0 | W&I = B2; first human writing band |
| 6 | 6.5 | 7.0 | 7.0 | 6.0 | 6.0–6.5 | 1st full mock; 200k words read; 120 h listening |
| 8 | 6.5–7.0 | 7.5 | 7.5 | 6.0–6.5 | 6.5 | Monthly human speaking mock ≥ 6.5 |
| 10 | 7.0 | 7.5–8.0 | 7.5–8.0 | 6.5 | 6.5–7.0 | W&I Task 2 = C1; 400k words read; 200 h listening |
| 11 | 7.0–7.5 | 8.0 | 8.0 | 6.5–7.0 | 7.0 | Two consecutive mocks ≥ 7.25 average → book the test |
| 12 | **7.5 (exam)** | 8.0 | 8.0 | 6.5–7.0 | 7.0 | — |

**Decision rules [J]:**
- **Behind by ≥ 0.5 band at month 6:** increase daily time (to ~2 h) or plan the exam for month 15–18; do not book at month 12.
- **Writing stuck at 6.0 for 2 checkpoints:** double human feedback frequency and switch half of Task 2 time to rewriting corrected essays.
- **L or R below 7.0 at month 8:** add 1 full listening or reading test per week and increase extensive input; look at question-type error tags.
- **Speaking stuck:** more human sessions, fewer app minutes; shadowing 10 min/day; repeat cue cards (task repetition).

## 9. First steps (this week)
1. **Diagnostic:** official free Listening familiarisation test; 2–3 official GT reading tasks; one timed Task 2 on Write & Improve; a recorded Part 2 cue card. Log all in Fluent365.
2. Buy one Cambridge IELTS GT book (keep 2 tests unused for months 11–12).
3. Choose 1 graded reader and 2 podcasts; start the input log.
4. Set Praktika/ChatGPT voice to examiner-style prompts (§2) for 15 min/day.
5. Book the first human writing correction for month 3.
